\section{Executive Summary: The \$100M+ Public Sector Opportunity}

Across judicial systems and municipal administrations worldwide, government operations continue to rely on physical paper files. In Latin America alone, paper dossiers represent a systemic vulnerability:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=3pt]
    \item \textbf{No Owner:} Once a folder leaves a police precinct, accountability is lost during inter-agency transit.
    \item \textbf{No Clock:} Peremptory constitutional deadlines (e.g., the 48-hour statutory flagrancy window) expire silently on desks.
    \item \textbf{No Memory:} Clerical errors lead officials to shred and reprint pages, leaving zero audit trail and causing catastrophic evidentiary dismissals at trial.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{The Opportunity for Foxit:} By integrating \textbf{Foxit Document Generation API}, \textbf{Foxit PDF Services API}, and a \textbf{SHA-256 Cryptographic Audit Chain}, we introduce an unalterable \emph{Traceability Layer}. Every document is born from certified templates, stamped with a cryptographic digest at second zero, and linked into an immutable blockchain-style audit log. This creates a scalable, high-margin SaaS model directly applicable to law enforcement, superior courts, municipal zoning, and public procurement.

Rather than using hiding tabs, our interactive platform renders both procedural trees \textbf{simultaneously side-by-side}, contrasting the paper failure mode with Foxit's cryptographic guarantee:

\begin{alertbox}[Tree 1: The Traditional Paper Regime (7 Points of Failure)]
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em,itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Precinct Record:} Paper drafted in ballpoint pen; zero cryptographic binding; vulnerable to page removal.
    \item \textbf{Seizure \& Custody:} Adhesive labels on evidence bags tear; no cross-referencing to parent files.
    \item \textbf{Transit Window:} File transported on motorcycle courier; no active digital custody; delays go unmonitored.
    \item \textbf{Peremptory Clock:} Deadline tracked in desk notebooks; 48-hour statutory window lapses unnoticed.
    \item \textbf{Material Error:} Typo in license plate leads officers to burn original sheets and reprint backdated pages.
    \item \textbf{Court Intake:} Physical folder misplaced in court mailroom; preliminary hearings adjourned.
    \item \textbf{Judicial Scrutiny:} Judge relies on smudged ink stamps; courtroom bogged down in authenticity debates.
\end{enumerate}
\end{alertbox}

\subsection{Cryptographic Vault \& Web Crypto Verification}
In the document vault, \textbf{the SHA-256 hash is the file name itself}:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash]
# Byte-for-byte SHA-256 verification
sha256sum site/vault/cdcd7e6bca653514e3624b4756892b8ac3bcfb541afa36977ef218d509c00218.pdf
# Result: cdcd7e6bca653514e3624b4756892b8ac3bcfb541afa36977ef218d509c00218  (100% MATCH)
\end{lstlisting}

Every client browser can verify document integrity in under 30 milliseconds using the native browser \texttt{window.crypto.subtle.digest('SHA-256', arrayBuffer)}, eliminating reliance on third-party verification servers.
